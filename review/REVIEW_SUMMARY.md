# Review summary

**Paper:** "Curvature, relative entropy, and global Sinkhorn convergence" (author: Anonymous).
**Status:** unrefereed candidate. This is a summary of an internal, AI-assisted review carried out between 1 and 3 November 2026. It is not human peer review.

Results are cited by their numbers in the final `manuscript.md`. Where an objection concerned the first version, the result is named by the number it now carries.

## 1. What was checked

The review had the following stages.

1. **Five reviewer roles on the first version:** an editor, a methodology reviewer, a domain reviewer, a perspective reviewer (probability, statistics and practice), and a devil's advocate. Each reviewer read the closest primary sources: Chizat, Delalande and Vaškevičius (CDV), Chiarini, Conforti, Greco and Tamanini (CCGT), and Aubin-Frankowski, Korba and Léger (AFKL). The reviewers also recomputed the key constants and asymptotics independently, several of them in high-precision arithmetic.
2. **A separate-model adversarial review.** "Astra", a Codex model distinct from the model that drafted the paper and ran the reviewer roles, audited each proof step, the prior-art comparison, and the most promising extension.
3. **Clean replays of the scripts.** The two original scripts were run from a fresh copy of the bundle, both normally and with Python's `-O` flag. Both runs gave zero failures, and every reported value matched the shipped outputs up to round-off. The script added in the revision, `code/revision_checks.py`, was replayed in the re-review and in the proof audit. The proof audit also confirmed that the script exits with an error when a failure is deliberately injected under `-O`.
4. **One revision round**, which also added new results (Section 3 below).
5. **A proof audit** of every new or rewritten proof, checked line by line against the primary sources.
6. **A re-review.** It checked that each requested change had been made, re-examined the positioning against CDV, CCGT, AFKL and Di Marino, and looked up all 21 DOIs and 8 arXiv identifiers.
7. **A second separate-model audit (Astra)** of the new and rewritten proofs.

## 2. Correctness

No stage found a counterexample to any stated theorem, either in the first version or in the revision. The reviewers re-derived Theorems 1 and 2, Corollary 5, Theorem 6 and Proposition 7 independently. The constants in Theorem 6 and Proposition 7 were reproduced to many digits.

The audits of the revised version found the following gaps, all of which were repaired.

* **Corollary 5, constant under CDV's (A2) and (A3).** The proof audit found that CDV's rescaling step in their Proposition 5.1 needs either a semiconcavity parameter of zero or a cost oscillation of at least one. In the remaining case the published constant $e\,c_\infty$ is not proved. The paper now uses $C=e\max\{c_\infty,1\}$, with a footnote explaining why. The bilinear case, CDV's (A1) case and the linear-in-$\lambda$ conclusion are unaffected.
* **Remark 4.2 (the mirror-descent reading).** Astra noted that inequality (10) gives relative strong convexity only on the family of couplings generated by bounded potentials, not on all couplings with the right source marginal. The remark now states that restriction and the invariance it needs. Any comparison with the optimal coupling is made conditional on a bounded optimal potential existing. The direct proof of (11) did not depend on this remark.
* **Corollary 9.** In the symmetric examples, CCGT's default initial potential is already optimal. The paper now says that the sharpness statement concerns non-optimal starting potentials, which CCGT's results allow. It also says that the trajectory argument of Section 7.2 applies to the family of Proposition 7.
* **Wording and scope.** Several sentences were tightened:
  * the abstract now qualifies the opening rate by the integrability assumptions;
  * "no single fixed source" became "no single fixed absolutely continuous source";
  * the claim that Theorem 2 confirms CDV's Remark 3.1 is now restricted to costs satisfying (7);
  * the results table separates the radius $L$ of Theorem 3 from the target radius $R$ of Corollary 4;
  * a misquoted CCGT Hessian hypothesis was corrected;
  * two CCGT propositions that had been run together are now stated separately;
  * Remark 5.2 now says that the source half-step contributes $1-D_\lambda$ times the target gain.

Two reviewers had estimated the true Poincaré constant of the cusp source (38) as about 0.52 to 0.60. The revision obtained 0.634 by a different numerical method, and an independent finite-element computation agreed to seven digits. The proof audit confirmed the numbers in Remark 7.2. These values are numerical estimates. The proved certificate is $C_P\le3/4$.

## 3. Main objections and how they were handled

**Overlap with CCGT.** Every reviewer, and Astra, made the same main objection. The first version described CCGT's results as giving only qualitative or indirect convergence. In fact CCGT's Propositions 1.8(i) and 1.9(i) state explicit contraction factors. Proposition 1.9(i) has the same rational form as Theorem 2, with the Talagrand constant $\tau$ in place of the Poincaré constant $C_P$. The paper was repositioned accordingly. Section 2.3 and Section 9.1, with a side-by-side table, now quote CCGT's statements and hypotheses. The paper's Poincaré result is described as a weakening of hypotheses at an equal or better constant, not a new rate. The weakening is a Poincaré inequality instead of a Talagrand inequality, first source derivatives of the cost instead of Hessian bounds, and one formula valid for every $\lambda$ and every bounded initial potential. The paper also records that Gaussian sources with compact targets are already covered by CCGT, and that CCGT treat settings the paper does not. The revision also corrected one point in the reviews, and the re-review confirmed it: CCGT's rates hold for all iterations $n\ge1$, not only eventually, and remain explicit for larger $\lambda$. The "all $\lambda$" gain is therefore stated modestly, as one formula valid for every $\lambda$.

**The mirror-descent route (AFKL).** The devil's advocate and the domain reviewer pointed out that, once the reverse entropy inequality (10) holds, the contraction (11) follows from AFKL's mirror-descent theorem. The paper now says so (Section 2.3 and Remark 4.2). It locates its own contribution in the step from (8) to (9) and (10), which gives a polynomial coefficient instead of AFKL's coefficient exponential in $1/\lambda$. Section 4.3 is presented as a self-contained alternative proof.

**Credit to CDV.** The curvature estimate behind Corollary 5 is CDV's Proposition 5.1. CDV's Remark 5.2 anticipates exactly the improvement that Corollary 5 delivers. The paper now credits both explicitly, and quotes CDV's Remark 3.1 exactly instead of paraphrasing it.

**Priority (Di Marino).** CDV report a 2022 announcement by S. Di Marino of a rate $1-\Theta(\lambda^2/c_\infty^2)$ for bounded Lipschitz costs when both marginals satisfy Poincaré inequalities. A 2021 seminar abstract by Di Marino also mentions counterexamples. Neither proof could be inspected; the 2022 workshop page was unavailable. The paper says that the exponent $\lambda^2$ was announced earlier, and claims only one marginal instead of two, the explicit constant, generality in the target, and unbounded costs. Priority for the exponent and for counterexamples is stated as uncertain.

**Bounded costs only.** In the first version Theorem 2 required a bounded cost, which excluded quadratic cost with Gaussian or other unbounded sources. Several reviewers saw this as the main limit on the paper's reach. The revision added Theorem 3 and Corollary 4. They cover any Poincaré source on $\mathbb R^d$, quadratic or bilinear cost, and any compactly supported target, including point clouds, for every $\lambda$. The two-sided exponential law is an example: it satisfies a Poincaré inequality but no Talagrand inequality.

**The sharpness results.** The devil's advocate argued that exponent sharpness follows from a simple power-law family of densities. Remark 7.1 now presents that family first, and the cusp source (38) as the single-source refinement. The revision also added Proposition 8 (no fixed absolutely continuous source attains order $\lambda^2$ in the two-atom model) and Corollary 9 (the exponent two is also sharp for Talagrand sources, in the setting of CCGT's Proposition 1.9(i)). The devil's advocate also noted that CDV's Remark 3.1 can be read in two ways. Section 7.6 answers one reading negatively and leaves the other open.

**Readability.** The editor and the perspective reviewer found the first version hard to read for non-specialists. The revision added a plain-English Section 1, a results table, a roadmap, the probabilistic reading in Remark 4.1, and the practical items in Section 5.2: an iteration count, a stopping certificate (33), and a bound for inexact iterations.

## 4. Remaining limitations

* **Population measures only.** The results concern the exact algorithm on continuous distributions. An empirical (sampled) source does not satisfy the Poincaré inequality (6), so the results do not apply directly to sampled data (Section 5.3 and open question 4).
* **No unbounded targets.** Theorem 3 needs a compactly supported target. Unbounded targets with quadratic cost, such as Gaussian to Gaussian, are not covered (open question 3).
* **Constants are not sharp.** On the family of Proposition 7, the guarantee is pessimistic by a factor of roughly 6 to 8 (Remark 7.2). Only the exponent two is shown to be sharp.
* **Conjecture in Remark 5.2.** The eventual factor $(B/(B+\lambda^2))^2$ is suggested by a linearisation but not proved (open question 1).
* **Bounded density on non-convex domains.** Whether a global rate linear in $\lambda$ holds in this case remains open (Section 7.6).
* **The scripts are floating-point checks, not proofs.** They support displayed formulas and constants over the tested ranges. No result in the paper depends on them.

## 5. What was not done

* No human peer review, and no independent human reproduction of the proofs or computations.
* No proof-assistant formalisation.
* No interval arithmetic. All numerical values are floating-point.
* A bounded literature search only: web search, arXiv full text, publisher pages, citation links, the Evidence Press catalogue and GitHub. No complete MathSciNet or zbMATH search was made.
* No enquiry to the authors of CDV or CCGT, or to Di Marino. The unpublished proofs behind the announced results were not obtained.

The earlier literature audit in `prior_art/novelty_report.md` predates the revision. Its description of the CCGT overlap is superseded by Sections 2.3 and 9.1 of the paper, as a note at the top of that report says.
